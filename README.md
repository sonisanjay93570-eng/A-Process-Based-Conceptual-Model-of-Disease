A Process-Based Conceptual Model of Disease Development, Cellular Change, Treatment Response, and Recurrence

1. Introduction

The human body is a complex biological system in which physical, chemical, and biological processes continuously interact. Energy is obtained and utilized, chemical reactions take place, cells grow and divide, tissues develop, and damaged cells undergo repair or removal.

The coordination of these processes contributes to the maintenance of normal biological functions. When particular processes become disrupted, various diseases may develop.

This conceptual model proposes examining disease not only through its currently observable condition or symptoms, but also through the processes and circumstances associated with its origin, development, treatment response, and possible recurrence.

The model is presented within the framework of Action-Flow Theory. Its purpose is not to replace established medical science, but to provide a broad, process-based conceptual framework whose usefulness can be evaluated through scientific evidence.

2. Central Idea

The central idea is that understanding the present condition of a biological system may require examining the processes that preceded it and the changes that occur afterward.

Disease development, biological changes, and post-treatment outcomes may be interconnected. Therefore, a comprehensive investigation of a disease may consider several aspects:

- Initial physiological conditions.
- Body temperature and thermal energy exchange.
- Cellular energy utilization.
- Chemical and biological interactions.
- Cell formation, division, and differentiation.
- Cellular damage, repair, and death.
- Disease progression and treatment response.
- Possible states of disease-associated cells remaining after treatment.
- Disease recurrence or the continuation of normal biological functions.

These factors do not have equal importance in every disease. Different diseases may arise through different mechanisms and require different approaches to investigation.

3. Proposed Process Sequence Under Action-Flow Theory

The general conceptual sequence is:

Physiological Conditions → Energy Flow and Utilization → Chemical and Biological Interactions → Cellular Changes → Changes in Tissue Function → Normal Biological Function or Disease → Treatment Response → Post-Treatment State.

This sequence is a preliminary conceptual representation, not a universal biological law.

Actual biological processes do not always occur in a simple, linear order. Multiple processes may occur simultaneously and influence one another.

For example, cellular activity depends on chemical reactions and biological signals. At the same time, cellular activity can alter the surrounding environment. Consequently, the relationships between causes and outcomes may be complex.

The model therefore proposes examining not only the sequence of events, but also the mechanisms involved, the strength of the relationships, and whether those relationships are causal or merely associated.

4. Thermal Energy and Physiological Conditions

The human body continuously uses energy and exchanges heat with its surroundings.

Body temperature is associated with many biological processes. Changes in temperature can influence enzyme activity, chemical reactions, and other physiological functions.

However, temperature alone cannot explain all biological activity. Nutrient availability, oxygen supply, hormones, genetic regulation, immune responses, cellular energy metabolism, and the surrounding cellular environment may also be important.

Within this model, thermal energy is considered one relevant aspect of physiological conditions. The model does not propose that thermal energy alone determines disease development or treatment outcomes.

Proposed Research Question

Can the combined measurement of temperature, energy utilization, and other relevant biological conditions provide additional information about cellular activity and disease progression?

The answer must be established through appropriate scientific evidence, measurements, and investigation of specific diseases.

5. Cellular Formation, Change, Damage, and Repair

Cells are the fundamental biological units of the human body. They perform specialized functions and contribute to the structure and operation of tissues and organs.

Cell division, differentiation, repair, and cell death are regulated biological processes.

Some dead cells and structures formed from cellular material contribute to protection or structural support. For example, dead cells in the outermost layer of human skin contribute to a protective barrier.

In other circumstances, damaged or unnecessary cells must be removed to maintain normal tissue function.

Therefore, cell death cannot be classified as universally harmful or universally beneficial. Its effects depend on the cell type, location, timing, and biological circumstances.

Proposed Concept

Cell formation, function, damage, repair, and death should be examined as interconnected aspects of biological processes.

This concept relates to established cell biology. To establish the additional scientific value of the present model, it will be necessary to identify specific measurable relationships or testable predictions beyond existing explanations.

6. Cancer and the Dormant State of Some Cancer Cells

Cancer provides an important example for investigating questions raised by this model.

Cancer involves abnormal cell growth and, in some cases, the ability of cancer cells to invade surrounding tissues or spread to other parts of the body.

Treatment may aim to eliminate cancer cells, control their growth, or reduce the disease. Outcomes depend on factors such as cancer type, stage, biological characteristics, and treatment response.

In some circumstances, cancer cells may remain in the body after treatment. Certain surviving cells can enter a dormant state or remain in a state of very slow proliferation. Some may later become active again and contribute to cancer recurrence.

However, not all cancers follow this pattern, and dormant cells are not the only possible cause of recurrence. Other biological and clinical factors may also contribute.

This leads to an important research question:

Can the study of surviving cancer cells, their biological states, and their surrounding environments improve the understanding or assessment of recurrence risk in selected cancers?

Cancer-cell dormancy is already an established area of scientific research. This model does not claim to discover a new mechanism or establish a new treatment. Instead, it proposes examining the relationships among relevant processes within a broader conceptual framework.

7. Understanding Treatment Within the Complete Disease Process

According to this model, the investigation of treatment outcomes should not necessarily end when treatment begins or when symptoms improve.

Where supported by scientific evidence and clinical circumstances, it may be useful to examine the underlying disease mechanisms, disease progression, treatment response, and subsequent biological state.

The proposed investigation sequence is:

1. Identify the nature of the disease and its possible causes.
2. Examine the relevant cellular and biological mechanisms.
3. Evaluate treatment effects using appropriate scientific evidence.
4. Monitor the condition after treatment.
5. Investigate surviving disease-associated cells or processes where relevant.
6. Assess known factors associated with recurrence risk.
7. Refine the conceptual model in light of the findings.

This sequence is a general research framework, not an individual treatment plan. Actual medical decisions depend on the specific disease and the advice of qualified healthcare professionals.

8. Understanding Disease Recurrence

A reduction in symptoms and the complete elimination of a disease are not always equivalent.

Some diseases may recur after treatment. The likelihood depends on the nature of the disease, treatment effectiveness, surviving disease-associated cells, genetic characteristics, and other factors.

Therefore, understanding treatment outcomes may require examining the changes that occurred during treatment, the conditions that remain afterward, and the known factors associated with recurrence.

However, it would be incorrect to conclude that every recurrence occurs because the initial conditions were not fully understood. Disease recurrence can have multiple causes, and current science does not provide a guaranteed method of preventing recurrence in every case.

The model proposes that the post-treatment state should be considered an important part of the overall disease process wherever relevant.

9. Proposed Questions for Scientific Testing

The usefulness of this conceptual model can be investigated through the following questions.

Question 1: Which initial biological conditions are associated with the development of a selected disease?

Question 2: Which changes in temperature, energy utilization, and chemical reactions are associated with the disease process?

Question 3: How are these changes related to cellular growth, damage, and repair?

Question 4: Which post-treatment measurements are useful for assessing disease status and, where possible, recurrence risk?

Question 5: Can a combined analysis of relevant biological factors provide additional information compared with examining each factor separately?

Question 6: Can the model generate a specific, testable prediction that distinguishes it from existing scientific models?

These questions should be investigated through relevant scientific literature, appropriate mathematical analysis, experimental or clinical data, and independent review.

Research involving human participants must follow applicable ethical requirements and clinical research standards.

10. Limitations of the Model

This work is a conceptual research proposal, not an established medical theory or a validated treatment method.

The broad sequence presented here does not, by itself, establish a new scientific law. Many relationships among energy utilization, cellular interactions, disease mechanisms, and treatment outcomes are already being investigated in established scientific fields.

The model's distinctiveness and usefulness can be assessed only when:

- Its claims are expressed in clear and measurable terms.
- Relevant existing scientific models are reviewed.
- Appropriate variables and methods are selected.
- Proposed relationships are tested using suitable evidence.
- Findings are independently evaluated and, where possible, reproduced.
- Negative results, uncertainties, and limitations are documented.

This model does not replace medical diagnosis, professional advice, or evidence-based treatment.

11. Conclusion

This conceptual model, developed within the framework of Action-Flow Theory, proposes examining physiological conditions, energy utilization, chemical and biological interactions, cellular changes, disease progression, treatment response, and post-treatment outcomes as interconnected aspects of biological processes.

The dormancy and possible reactivation of some cancer cells provide a relevant example of why post-treatment biological conditions may be important in understanding selected diseases.

Nevertheless, a broad conceptual idea becomes scientifically useful only when it is translated into specific research questions, measurable variables, and testable predictions.

The purpose of this model is not to present one universal explanation or treatment for all diseases. Its purpose is to encourage systematic examination of relationships among biological processes and to provide a preliminary framework for scientific discussion and investigation.

Core Principle

Understanding a disease may require examining not only its present state, but also its origin, development, treatment response, and the biological changes that occur afterward.

“Experience is an idea, not a claim.”
